topic: Sulfonylureas and Hypoglycaemia Vigilance
source_note: paraphrased summary; verify against current ADA/ICMR guidance
---
Sulfonylureas act as secretagogues by binding to pancreatic beta-cell potassium channels to stimulate sustained insulin release independent of circulating blood glucose levels. They provide rapid and robust initial HbA1c reductions between 1.0% and 1.5% and offer an economical therapeutic alternative in low-resource settings. However, their mechanism inherently creates significant risks of treatment-emergent hypoglycemia and modest weight gain. Patients with pre-existing hypoglycemic episodes, irregular meal routines, or kidney impairment are particularly vulnerable to protracted low-sugar events. When considered in high-risk groups, modern agents with shorter half-lives such as glimepiride or gliclazide are preferred over chlorpropamide. Structured patient education regarding early neuroglycopenic symptoms, continuous access to fast-acting simple carbohydrates, and home self-monitoring are indispensable preconditions before recommending this class.
