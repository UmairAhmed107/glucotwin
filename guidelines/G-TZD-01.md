topic: Thiazolidinediones and Insulin Sensitization
source_note: paraphrased summary; verify against current ADA/ICMR guidance
---
Thiazolidinediones (such as pioglitazone) are peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-gamma (PPAR-gamma) agonists that augment peripheral insulin sensitivity in adipose, skeletal muscle, and hepatic tissues. They produce durable glycemic improvements, typically achieving average HbA1c reductions between 0.8% and 1.2% with virtually zero risk of hypoglycemia when prescribed without insulin secretagogues. Furthermore, they exert beneficial effects on lipid profiles and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Nevertheless, clinical utility is limited by fluid retention, peripheral edema, and weight gain, making this class contraindicated in patients with symptomatic New York Heart Association Class III or IV heart failure. Long-term usage also elevates postmenopausal fracture risks and necessitates bone health vigilance. Careful candidate selection and baseline cardiac history evaluation are essential prior to therapy commencement.
