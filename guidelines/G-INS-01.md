topic: Basal Insulin Initiation and Hypoglycaemia Management
source_note: paraphrased summary; verify against current ADA/ICMR guidance
---
Basal insulin provides consistent background insulinemia over 24 hours to effectively suppress hepatic gluconeogenesis and control fasting hyperglycemia. It remains the most potent glycemic therapy available, demonstrating unconstrained HbA1c lowering capability depending on titration, commonly lowering HbA1c by 1.5% to 2.5% in individuals with severe glycemic elevation or symptomatic catabolism. The primary therapeutic hazards associated with exogenous insulin are acute hypoglycemia and progressive weight gain. Structured self-monitoring of blood glucose, particularly fasting readings, is essential to guide systematic dosage titration safely. In individuals with documented recurrent hypoglycemia, unawareness of autonomic warnings, or fluctuating nutritional intake, long-acting basal analog formulations with peakless pharmacokinetic profiles are preferred over traditional intermediate human neutral protamine Hagedorn (NPH) insulins to minimize nocturnal hypoglycemic events.
