topic: Carbohydrate Quality, Composition, and Meal Timing
source_note: paraphrased summary; verify against current ADA/ICMR guidance
---
Nutritional strategies prioritize carbohydrate quality and distribution rather than uniform severe restriction. Substituting refined grains, sugar-sweetened beverages, and ultra-processed starches with nutrient-dense legumes, intact whole grains, and non-starchy vegetables slows enzymatic digestive transit and attenuates postprandial glucose surges. Incorporating dietary fiber, healthy fats, and adequate dietary protein alongside complex carbohydrates reduces the overall glycemic load and prolongs satiety. Furthermore, chronobiological meal timing influences insulin sensitivity, which typically peaks earlier in the diurnal cycle; consuming substantial caloric and carbohydrate allotments earlier in the day rather than late at night optimizes glucose disposal. Modest, sustainable carbohydrate moderations (typically 15% to 30% reduction from excess baselines) prevent dramatic glycemic spikes while supporting long-term adherence without triggering nutritional deficiencies.
