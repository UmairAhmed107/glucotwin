topic: Metformin as First-Line Pharmacotherapy
source_note: paraphrased summary; verify against current ADA/ICMR guidance
---
Metformin remains the foundational first-line oral antihyperglycemic medication for individuals diagnosed with type 2 diabetes unless specific contraindications exist. It primarily acts by diminishing hepatic glucose production and secondarily by improving peripheral insulin sensitivity without promoting weight gain or intrinsic hypoglycemia. In general clinical population trials, monotherapy typically achieves an anticipated average HbA1c reduction between 1.0% and 1.5%. Clinicians must exercise caution and evaluate renal function before initiation; while safe in mild impairment, dosage review is recommended when estimated glomerular filtration rates drop below 45 mL/min/1.73m², and discontinuation is warranted below 30 mL/min/1.73m². Gradual dose titration and administration with substantial meals substantially diminish gastrointestinal adverse effects such as nausea or abdominal cramping.
